Postdoctoral Research (2022–2025)

Global Plate Tectonics Activity – as part of the MEET Project

Supervisors: Prof. Stephan Sobolev
Institutions: GFZ Potsdam
Keywords: Mantle convection, ASPECT, plate tectonics, tectonic–erosion interactions, Earth history

Within the MEET framework, I investigate the evolution of global plate-tectonic activity and its first-order interactions with major erosional episodes in Earth’s history. Most notably Snowball Earth–style glaciations that can affect the sediment flux and lubricate the plate boundaries.

Evolution of sediments proxy, number of passive margins, glaciations over time and supercontinent cycle phases.

PhD Thesis Project (2018–2022)

The nature of the North–South change of tectonic shortening in Central Andes

Advisor: Prof. Stephan Sobolev
Keywords: Andes, geodynamics, subduction, shortening, plateau

The subduction of the oceanic Nazca Plate under the continental South American Plate has been active since at least 180 Ma. However, the cause of the initiation of the shortening of the Andes during the last 50 Myr, and the reasons behind the spatial and temporal variations in shortening magnitude, remain debated. Our models, built with ASPECT, test how variations in the overriding plate’s strength control trench rollback and deformation localization across the Altiplano–Puna plateau.

2D subduction model of Central Andes at 21°S

References:
Faccenna, C. et al. (2017). Initiation of the Andean orogeny by lower mantle subduction. Earth Planet. Sci. Lett.
Oncken, O. et al. (2012). Strain accumulation controls failure of a plate boundary zone. Geochem. Geophys. Geosyst.
Sobolev, S. V. et al. (2006). Mechanism of the Andean orogeny. In The Andes. Springer.

M2 Research Project (2017)

Earthquakes impact on GRACE gravimetric signal at global scale

Advisors: Dr. Maxime Mouyen, Prof. Philippe Steer, Prof. Laurent Longuevergne

The GRACE satellite mission measures monthly variations in Earth's gravity field caused by mass redistribution. This study models co-seismic gravity perturbations (GCMT 2002–2014) and compares them with GRACE data. Large earthquakes (Mw ≥ 8) show consistent signatures between modeled and observed signals.

Mechanisms responsible for co-seismic gravity change (modified from Tanaka, 2017)

Cumulative gravimetric trend map (2002–2014) highlighting earthquake-related signals near Sumatra and Japan.

M2 Research Project (2016)

Topographic evolution of Deccan Traps using DEM modeling

Advisors: Prof. Claudio Faccenna, Dr. Andrea Sembroni

The Deccan Traps provide a natural laboratory for studying lithospheric processes in India. GIS-based modeling shows that regional tilt is best explained by lithospheric thinning beneath the Western Ghats, consistent with isostatic control.

Deflection profiles showing variable elastic thicknesses explaining inland topography in central Deccan.

M1 Research Project (2015)

Paleomagnetic study of the Upper Jurassic Qitianling granite (South China)

Advisors: Prof. Yan Chen, Prof. Hongsheng Liu

Paleomagnetic analysis of the Qitianling granite constrains the timing of the Mongol–Okhotsk Ocean closure. The pluton cooled and acquired its remanence around 150 Ma, implying the ocean was still open in the Late Jurassic.

Stereographic projection of paleomagnetic poles indicating crustal shortening.

Publication:
Liu, H., Martelet, G., Wang, B., et al. (2018). Incremental emplacement of the late Jurassic mid-crustal Qitianling pluton. J. Geophys. Res.: Solid Earth.
