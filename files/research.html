PhD Thesis Project (2018–2022)

The nature of the North–South change of tectonic shortening in Central Andes at Altiplano–Puna latitudes: a thermomechanical modeling approach

Advisor: Prof. Stephan Sobolev

Keywords: Andes, geodynamics, subduction, shortening, orogen, plateau

The subduction of the oceanic Nazca Plate under the continental South American Plate has been active since at least 180 Ma. However, the cause of the initiation of the shortening of the Andes during the last 50 Myr, and the reasons behind the spatial and temporal variations in shortening magnitude, remain debated. In the case of the Central Andes, the South American plate is advancing westwards, forcing the trench to retreat and the Nazca plate to roll back. However, paleo-reconstructions show that trench retreat has slowed over the past 50 Myr, producing Andean shortening as the South American plate overtakes the trench. A possible cause of this slowdown is the anchoring of the slab in the lower mantle (Faccenna et al., 2017). Our models, built with ASPECT, test how variations in the overriding plate’s strength control trench rollback and deformation localization across the Altiplano–Puna plateau.

M2 Research Project (2017)

Earthquakes impact on GRACE gravimetric signal at global scale

Advisors: Dr. Maxime Mouyen, Prof. Philippe Steer, Prof. Laurent Longuevergne

Keywords: GRACE, scaling laws, earthquakes, gravity change

The GRACE satellite mission (Gravity Recovery and Climate Experiment) measures monthly variations in Earth's gravity field caused by mass redistribution. While hydrology and ice melting dominate this signal, large earthquakes can also produce measurable changes. This study models the co-seismic gravity perturbations associated with global seismicity (GCMT catalog, 2002–2014). Modeled signals are compared with GRACE data from CSR (Austin, TX). Large earthquakes (Mw ≥ 8) show consistent signatures between models and GRACE observations.

M2 Research Project (2016)

Topographic evolution of Deccan Traps using DEM modeling

Advisors: Prof. Claudio Faccenna, Dr. Andrea Sembroni

Keywords: Western Ghats, flexure, dynamic, isostasy, topography

The Deccan Traps provide a key natural laboratory for studying lithospheric processes in India. This project used GIS-based modeling to analyze the long-wavelength tilting of the plateau and evaluate the relative role of flexural unloading, dynamic topography, and isostasy. Results show that the regional tilt is best explained by lithospheric thinning beneath the Western Ghats escarpment, consistent with isostatic control rather than erosion-driven flexure.

M1 Research Project (2015)

Paleomagnetic study of the Upper Jurassic Qitianling granite (South China)

Advisors: Prof. Yan Chen, Prof. Hongsheng Liu

Keywords: Qitianling, Mongol-Okhotsk, paleomagnetism, tectonics

Paleomagnetic analysis of the Qitianling granite constrains the timing of the Mongol–Okhotsk Ocean closure. Magnetic fabric and demagnetization data show that the pluton cooled and acquired its remanence around 150 Ma. Comparison of the resulting South China Block pole with those of North China and Siberia implies that the ocean was still open in the Late Jurassic, with a minimum width of ~500 km.

Stereographic projection of paleomagnetic poles for SCB, NCB, and Eurasia, indicating crustal shortening.
