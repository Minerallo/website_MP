1 August 2026 — New Paper in Earth and Planetary Science Letters

Image credit: Lea Pennacchioni

Our new paper, “Geodynamic Controls on the Habitable Basement of Shallow Coral Reefs at Rifted Continental Margins”, led by Zhibin Lei and which I co-authored, has been published in Earth and Planetary Science Letters.

Using coupled thermo-mechanical geodynamic and surface-process models, the study investigates how the evolution of rifted continental margins controls the creation of shallow bathymetric areas that can provide a suitable basement for coral reef development.

The results show that both rifting and passive-margin evolution can generate habitable bathymetric zones, with important differences between narrow and wide rift systems. They also highlight the major role of erosion and sedimentation in shaping the long-term extent of potentially habitable reef basement, connecting geodynamics, surface processes, and marine biodiversity.

Congratulations to Lei for leading this work! Read the paper on the Earth and Planetary Science Letters website.

June 2026 — New Paper in Frontiers in Earth Science

Image credit: Madhusudan Sharma

Our new paper, “Subduction Dynamics driven by Crustal Heterogeneities in the Subducting Plate”, led by Madhusudan Sharma and which I co-authored, has been published in Frontiers in Earth Science.

The study partly developed through a broader collaboration and scientific discussions at GFZ Potsdam and during meetings in Barcelona and Madrid, focusing on numerical models of subduction dynamics and the role of inherited structures in the incoming oceanic plate.

Using 2D geodynamic models, the study investigates how crustal heterogeneities within a subducting plate influence slab geometry, trench migration, back-arc deformation, and slab breakoff. The results show that inherited structures in the incoming plate can strongly affect the evolution of an entire subduction system.

Congratulations to Madhu for leading this work! Read the paper on the Frontiers in Earth Science website.

14 February 2026 — New Paper in JGR: Solid Earth

Our new paper, “Numerical Modeling of Purely Active (Plume-Produced) Continental Rifting and Break-Up”, led by Alexander Koptev and co-authored with Alessio Lavecchia, Sierd Cloetingh, Enrico Marzotto, Sascha Brune, István Kovács, Magdala Tesauro, Fred Beekman, Qin Wang, Stephan V. Sobolev, and colleagues, has been published in Journal of Geophysical Research: Solid Earth.

Using high-resolution ASPECT thermo-mechanical models, we systematically test whether mantle plumes alone can generate continental break-up without far-field tectonic forces. Our results show that purely active break-up is possible, but requires exceptionally buoyant plumes and a thermally weakened lithosphere. This suggests that most Phanerozoic continental break-up events likely involved a combination of plume activity and external tectonic stresses.

12 February 2026 — New Paper in Tectonics

Our new paper, “The Impact of Orogenic Inheritance on Rifted Margin Formation”, led by Kai Li and co-authored with Sascha Brune, Zoltán Erdős, Anne Glerum, and myself, has been published in Tectonics. Using coupled ASPECT–FastScape numerical models, we quantitatively isolate the roles of structural, thermal, and compositional inheritance in rift evolution. I contributed by implementing in ASPECT a method to reset accumulated plastic strain after a prescribed time interval, allowing us to test the specific impact of structural inheritance during the transition from orogeny to rifting.

9 November 2025 — DFG Project Funded: Slab Avalanching and Basin Evolution

Exciting news, our DFG proposal has been successfully funded! Together with Dr. Giridas Maiti and TT. Prof. Dr. Nevena Andrić-Tomašević from the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT), I will co-supervise a new project investigating whether slab-avalanching-induced mantle flow accelerates post-rift subsidence in extensional basins. The project combines thermomechanical geodynamic modelling (ASPECT) with surface process simulations (FastScape) to quantify the interplay between deep mantle dynamics and erosion–sedimentation feedbacks in basin evolution. Our work will also integrate fault analysis toolbox (Fatbox) to track rift development and sedimentary architecture through time. This study aims to unravel how the coupling between slab avalanching, mantle flow, and surface processes shapes post-rift landscapes and basin subsidence across the globe.
