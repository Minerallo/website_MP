News & Highlights

5 November 2025 — Invited Seminar at Charles University, Prague

I was invited by the Department of Geophysics at Charles University in Prague to give a seminar titled “Geodynamic Modelling of Tectonic Processes on Earth and Venus Across Scales.” During my talk, I explored how planetary-scale mantle convection and lithospheric feedbacks control the long-term evolution of plate tectonics. You can find the full program on the seminar website.

21 October 2025 — EOS Research Spotlight

Our recent work was featured in an EOS Research Spotlight titled “Seismic Anisotropy Reveals Deep-Mantle Dynamics.” The article highlights how shear wave anisotropy reveals flow organization in the lowermost mantle, providing new insights into thermochemical pile dynamics and mantle fabric evolution. Read it on the EOS website.

13 October 2025 — Invited Talk at Durham University, UK

I was delighted to visit the Department of Earth Sciences at Durham University, where I opened the autumn seminar series with a talk on geodynamic modelling of tectonic activity on Earth and Venus. I discussed how mantle convection simulations help us understand planetary evolution and the transition between stagnant-lid and mobile-lid regimes. The event was featured on Bluesky.

4 October 2025 — New Paper in Geochemistry, Geophysics, Geosystems (G³)

Our new paper, “Lowermost Mantle Flow at Thermochemical Piles Constrained by Shear Wave Anisotropy”, has been published in G³ (AGU). We combined 3D global convection models in ASPECT with fabric simulations (ECO-MAN) to understand how flow at the base of the mantle generates seismic anisotropy at the margins of large low-shear-velocity provinces. The paper is available on the AGU Publications website.

15–19 September 2025 — Biogeodynamics Online School

As part of the COST Action EUROBIG, I co-organized and taught during the “Introduction to Geodynamic Modelling” online school. I presented lectures and Geodynamics exercises on coupling between ASPECT and FastScape, showing how surface processes and lithosphere dynamics interact through geological time. Learn more on the Biogeodynamics Network website.

9–20 June 2025 — ASPECT Hackathon, Garden City (Utah, USA)

I participated in the ASPECT developers’ hackathon in Utah, focusing on implementing a more efficient way to communicate surface mesh deformation between ASPECT and FastScape C++. This work aims to improve the coupling between global plate tectonics models and surface evolution models. Read about the event on the ASPECT community page.

2 June 2025 — Invited Talk at COMTEC (Argentina)

I gave a virtual invited talk for the COMTEC seminar series in Argentina titled “De-coding Plate Tectonics through Andean Geodynamics.” I discussed how subduction dynamics and crustal inheritance shape the evolution of the Andes. Watch the recording on YouTube.

26 May 2025 — New Paper in Geophysical Research Letters

Our collaborative study led by Poulami Roy and Bernhard Steinberger has been published in Geophysical Research Letters! The paper, titled “Modeling Anisotropic Signature of Slab-Induced Mantle Plumes from Thermochemical Piles in the Lowermost Mantle”, investigates how slab–pile interactions shape the formation and seismic fabric of mantle plumes. Read the full paper on the AGU website.

27 April – 2 May 2025 — EGU General Assembly, Vienna

I presented my abstract “Subduction network connectivity — A comparison between Earth and Venus.” This contribution compared plate boundaries dynamics across rocky planets, highlighting how mantle convection and lithospheric weakening processes may explain the lack of plate tectonics on Venus. Abstract available on the EGU website.

5 April 2025 — New Paper in JGR: Solid Earth

Excited to share a new publication led by Bernhard Steinberger, co-authored with Poulami Roy, Matteo Paul Jopke, and myself, in Journal of Geophysical Research: Solid Earth. The study, titled “Why Are Plume Excess Temperatures Much Less Than the Temperature Drop Across the Lowermost-Mantle Thermal Boundary Layer?”, explores how mantle viscosity, thermal structure, and plume dynamics explain the discrepancy between expected and observed plume excess temperatures. I contributed by helping design and benchmark the ASPECT models of deep mantle plumes. Available on the AGU Publications website.
